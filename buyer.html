Market Position & Growth

Market position and growth opportunity

How the two cottages sit against the local market, and where the runway is.

The Wakatipu Basin is one of the country’s tightest short-stay markets. Demand holds up through the year on the strength of Queenstown and Arrowtown tourism, while supply is held back by the district plan, which lets a property like this take paying guests for only 90 nights a year without a specific consent. That scarcity keeps nightly rates firm: across the local comparable set, one-bedroom homes clear a median of about $220 a night and two-bedroom homes about $366, with the stronger listings well above that.

Where the cottages sit

Stone Cottage is already trading at the top of its bracket, at an average rate of about $387 a night, close to the 90th percentile for one-bedroom homes locally, and around 78% occupancy since it opened in December 2025. For the newer of the two listings to launch straight into a premium position is a strong sign. Arrow Junction (Cream) Cottage is the more interesting case. As a two-bedroom unit it should sit well above Stone, yet its average rate of about $276 is currently below the bottom quartile of the two-bedroom market, at about 61% occupancy. That is not a fault in the cottage. It is the longer-established of the two, and its rate was set conservatively while occupancy built; it is now climbing back toward where the market clears, which points straight to the upside.

Opportunity for growth

The clearest opportunity is Cream Cottage’s rate. The two-bedroom market median sits near $366 and the upper quartile near $533, both well above its current $276, and there is room to lift the rate toward the market without moving beyond where it already clears. Its occupancy has similar headroom toward the high-70s Stone already runs at. Both cottages also still take most bookings through the platforms, which charge around 15% commission. Direct bookings, currently about 4% for Stone and 11% for Cream, carry none, so every direct booking lands straight on the bottom line. Underpinning all of it is the VA365 consent, which lets both cottages trade every night of the year rather than the 90 the plan allows by default. The room to grow nights and rate is already consented and in place.

Beyond the accommodation

The upside is not only in the nightly rate. The entire property lends itself to exclusive-use hire for weddings and small events. Two self-contained cottages, the studio and gallery, and private grounds all sit on a single freehold title, so a wedding party or a retreat group can take the place in its entirety, which attracts a premium over STA (Short Term Accommodation). Subject to council approval, the property could be offered in its entirety on a case by case basis at a premium rate.

The studio and gallery building carries its own commercial consent, currently framed around a previous owner’s use. Planning advice on file sets out that those conditions can be varied, with council approval, to operate a small café, gallery and bike hire from the building as a compact tourist node, or the space can be leased to an operator instead. The property sits directly on the Queenstown Trail, the Arrow River Bridges Ride that runs from Arrowtown toward the Gibbston Valley, so it is well placed for passing cycle and visitor trade. The network is about to grow again, with the Kawarau Gorge Trail opening in September or October 2026: southernlakestrails.nz/kawarau-gorge-trail.

The property draws water from a nearby spring with an allowed take of up to 24,000 litres a day, well beyond current property requirements.

Cottage review

How each cottage is performing against its market, at the latest pricing review.

Stone Cottage — outperforming

Running near 78% occupancy at about $387 a night, close to the top of the one-bedroom bracket, and filling the forward calendar at roughly 1.5 to 1.9 times the market’s pace. The pricing engine rates it as outperforming its market — a proven premium performer with room to keep lifting peak and weekend rates.

Cream Cottage — catching up fast

Trailing revenue is up about 59% year on year and its nightly rate about 23%, at roughly 61% occupancy and climbing over the last quarter. At about $276 it still sits below the two-bedroom market’s lower quartile, so there is clear rate headroom toward the $366 median and $533 upper quartile as its pricing is lifted toward where the market clears.

Together

One cottage proves the ceiling and the other shows the runway. Both are trading ahead of the neighbourhood; Stone is the finished article on rate and occupancy, while Cream is the value still being unlocked.
